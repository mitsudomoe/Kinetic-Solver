    Theory¶

    This page summarises the mathematics the code implements. Equation numbers such as (3.9) refer to Perthame & Simeoni (2001); see References.

    The Saint-Venant system¶

    With \(h \ge 0\) the water height, \(u\) the velocity, \(q = hu\) the discharge, \(Z(x)\) the bottom and \(g\) the gravity,

    \[ \begin{aligned} \partial_t h + \partial_x (hu) &= 0, \\ \partial_t (hu) + \partial_x\!\left(hu^2 + \tfrac{g}{2} h^2\right) &= -g\,h\,\partial_x Z . \end{aligned} \]

    The system is hyperbolic for \(h > 0\), with wave speeds \(u \pm \sqrt{gh}\); it loses strict hyperbolicity at \(h = 0\) (dry areas). Two properties matter for the numerics:

    • Steady states at rest. Every state with \(u = 0\) and \(h + Z = H\) constant (a lake at rest) is steady: the pressure gradient exactly balances the bottom slope. A scheme that does not reproduce this balance creates spurious currents over any non-flat bottom.
    • Energy. The energy \(E = \tfrac12 h u^2 + \tfrac{g}{2} h^2 + g Z h\) is conserved by smooth solutions and decreases across shocks: \(\partial_t E + \partial_x\!\big(u (E + \tfrac{g}{2} h^2)\big) \le 0\).

    Kinetic formulation¶

    The kinetic approach describes the water as a density \(f(t, x, \xi)\) of fictitious particles moving at velocity \(\xi\). The macroscopic quantities are its moments, and at equilibrium the density is a Maxwellian

    \[ M(h, \xi - u) = \sqrt{h}\;\chi\!\left(\frac{\xi - u}{\sqrt{h}}\right), \qquad \chi(\omega) = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{\pi \sqrt{g}} \left(1 - \frac{\omega^2}{2g}\right)_+^{1/2}, \]

    which satisfies

    \[ \int M \, d\xi = h, \qquad \int \xi M \, d\xi = hu, \qquad \int \xi^2 M \, d\xi = hu^2 + \tfrac{g}{2} h^2 . \]

    The Saint-Venant system is then equivalent to the single linear transport equation

    \[ \partial_t f + \xi\, \partial_x f - g\, \partial_x Z\; \partial_\xi f = Q, \]

    where the collision term \(Q\) has zero mass and momentum and relaxes \(f\) towards \(M\). The bottom acts as a force on the particles: along a trajectory their mechanical energy \(\tfrac12 \xi^2 + g Z\) is conserved.

    The half-ellipse \(\chi\) is not arbitrary: it is the density that minimises the kinetic energy \(\int \big(\tfrac12 \xi^2 f + \tfrac{\pi^2 g^2}{6} f^3 + g Z f\big)\, d\xi\) under the constraints of given \(h\) and \(hu\) (Lemma 2.3). This choice is what gives the scheme its entropy inequality. Its compact support, \(|\xi - u| \le \sqrt{2gh}\), also bounds the particle speeds, which is where the CFL condition comes from.

    The scheme with reflections¶

    The domain is divided into cells \(C_i\) of width \(\Delta x\), with piecewise constant bottom \(Z_i\). At each time step the density is reset to the local Maxwellian \(M_i^n\) ("all collisions at once") and transported upwind:

    \[ f_i^{n+1}(\xi) = M_i^n(\xi) - \frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\,\xi\left(M^-_{i+1/2}(\xi) - M^+_{i-1/2}(\xi)\right). \]

    The bottom jump at an interface is treated as a potential barrier. Write \(\Delta Z_{i+1/2} = Z_{i+1} - Z_i\) and \(\Delta Z_{i-1/2} = Z_{i-1} - Z_i\): the jump seen from cell \(i\). Particles leaving cell \(i\) through its right interface with velocity \(\xi > 0\) are counted directly. The particles arriving from the right (\(\xi < 0\)) are of two kinds:

    • particles of cell \(i\) that tried to climb the step without enough energy, and come back reflected with velocity \(-\xi\) (when \(\xi^2 \le 2g\,\Delta Z_{i+1/2}\));
    • particles of cell \(i+1\) that crossed the step, their speed changed by the energy balance \(\tfrac12 \xi^2 = \tfrac12 \xi'^2 + g\,\Delta Z_{i+1/2}\).

    This gives, for \(\xi \le 0\),

    \[ M_{i+1/2}(\xi) = M_i(-\xi)\,\mathbb{1}_{\xi^2 \le 2g\Delta Z_{i+1/2}} + M_{i+1}\!\left(-\sqrt{\xi^2 - 2g\Delta Z_{i+1/2}}\right)\mathbb{1}_{\xi^2 \ge 2g\Delta Z_{i+1/2}}, \]

    and symmetrically at \(x_{i-1/2}\) for \(\xi \ge 0\), with \(M_{i-1}\) and \(+\sqrt{\xi^2 - 2g\Delta Z_{i-1/2}}\). The interface densities are then

    \[ M^-_{i+1/2} = M_i\,\mathbb{1}_{\xi \ge 0} + M_{i+1/2}\,\mathbb{1}_{\xi \le 0}, \qquad M^+_{i-1/2} = M_{i-1/2}\,\mathbb{1}_{\xi \ge 0} + M_i\,\mathbb{1}_{\xi \le 0}. \]

    Taking moments gives the finite volume scheme

    \[ U_i^{n+1} = U_i^n - \frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\left(F^-_{i+1/2} - F^+_{i-1/2}\right), \qquad F^\pm = \int \xi \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ \xi \end{pmatrix} M^\pm(\xi)\, d\xi . \]

    The source term never appears as a volume term: it is entirely carried by the difference between the flux seen from the left of an interface, \(F^-\), and the flux seen from its right, \(F^+\) (interfacial upwind sources).

    Properties¶

    Under the CFL condition (3.9)

    \[ \Delta t \, \max_i \left(|u_i^n| + \sqrt{2 g h_i^n}\right) \le \Delta x, \]

    the scheme (Theorem 3.2)

    1. keeps \(h_i^n \ge 0\): \(f_i^{n+1}\) is a convex combination of non-negative densities;
    2. satisfies a discrete in-cell entropy (energy) inequality;
    3. preserves every lake at rest: when \(u_i = 0\) and \(h_i + Z_i\) is constant, \(M^-_{i+1/2} = M^+_{i-1/2} = M_i\), so nothing moves.

    The mass flux is single-valued: \(F^{h,-}_{i+1/2} = F^{h,+}_{i+1/2}\) (Remark 3.1), so water is conserved exactly. The momentum fluxes differ across a step, which is how the bottom force enters.

    Closed-form fluxes¶

    With the dimensionless numbers

    \[ Fr_i = \frac{u_i}{\sqrt{2 g h_i}}, \qquad K^-_{i+1/2} = \frac{\Delta Z_{i+1/2}}{h_i}, \qquad K^+_{i+1/2} = \frac{\Delta Z_{i+1/2}}{h_{i+1}}, \]

    and \((a)_+ = \max(a, 0)\), \((a)_- = \max(-a, 0)\), the flux seen from cell \(i\) is

    \[ \begin{aligned} F^{h,-}_{i+1/2} &= \frac{2\sqrt{2g}}{\pi}\left[h_i^{3/2}\left(II^h_1 + II^h_2\right) + h_{i+1}^{3/2}\, II^h_3\right], \\ F^{q,-}_{i+1/2} &= \frac{4g}{\pi}\left[h_i^{2}\left(II^q_1 + II^q_2\right) + h_{i+1}^{2}\, II^q_3\right], \end{aligned} \]

    where, writing \(s(\omega) = (1 - \omega^2)_+^{1/2}\),

    Integral Particles Definition
    \(II^h_1\), \(II^q_1\) of cell \(i\), leaving to the right \(\int_{\omega \ge -Fr_i} (\omega + Fr_i)^{k}\, s(\omega)\, d\omega\)
    \(II^h_2\), \(II^q_2\) of cell \(i\), reflected by an upward step \(\mp\int_{-Fr_i}^{\sqrt{(K^-)_+} - Fr_i} (\omega + Fr_i)^{k}\, s(\omega)\, d\omega\)
    \(II^h_3\) of cell \(i+1\), crossing to the left \(\int_{\omega \le -\sqrt{(K^+)_-} - Fr_{i+1}} (\omega + Fr_{i+1})\, s(\omega)\, d\omega\)
    \(II^q_3\) of cell \(i+1\), crossing to the left \(-\int_{\omega \le -\sqrt{(K^+)_-} - Fr_{i+1}} (\omega + Fr_{i+1})\sqrt{(\omega + Fr_{i+1})^2 + K^+}\; s(\omega)\, d\omega\)

    with \(k = 1\) for the mass (\(h\)) and \(k = 2\) for the momentum (\(q\)); the sign of \(II_2\) is \(-\) for the mass and \(+\) for the momentum. All of them have elementary primitives except \(II^q_3\) (Remark 4.1), which must be computed numerically (see Implementation). The flux seen from the right, \(F^+\), follows from the same formulas by the symmetry \(x \to -x\).

    The momentum interface law (Ersoy, 2026)¶

    M. Ersoy revisited the scheme in a discontinuous Galerkin setting and identified the macroscopic law it selects at a bottom jump. The defect between the two one-sided momentum fluxes is exactly

    \[ F^{q,-}_{i+1/2} - F^{q,+}_{i+1/2} = g\,\Delta Z_{i+1/2}\; h^{\text{jump}}_{i+1/2}, \qquad h^{\text{jump}}_{i+1/2} \ge 0, \]

    where \(h^{\text{jump}}\) is an effective interface height built from three groups of particles. With \(\alpha = \sqrt{2g\lvert\Delta Z\rvert}\), \(M^-\) the Maxwellian of the left cell and \(M^+\) that of the right cell, for an upward step (\(\Delta Z > 0\)):

    \[ h^{\text{jump}} = \frac{2}{g\Delta Z}\int_0^\alpha \xi^2 M^-(\xi)\,d\xi + 2\int_\alpha^\infty \frac{\xi\, M^-(\xi)}{\xi + \sqrt{\xi^2 - \alpha^2}}\,d\xi + 2\int_0^\infty \frac{\xi\, M^+(-\xi)}{\sqrt{\xi^2 + \alpha^2} + \xi}\,d\xi : \]

    reflected particles, particles climbing the step, and particles coming down it. The downward case swaps the roles of the two cells. At rest, \(h^{\text{jump}}\) reduces to the mean height \((h_i + h_{i+1})/2\), which is the hydrostatic pressure jump. For polynomial degree 0 the discontinuous Galerkin scheme of that paper is exactly the finite volume scheme above. The test suite checks this identity numerically.
